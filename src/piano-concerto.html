Piano Concerto in C-sharp minor

Program note

The idea behind this concerto was simple. As a composer who spends much of my time at the piano, I have always been drawn to the concerto repertoire, and after years of listening to and performing Rachmaninoff, Scriabin, Prokofiev, Schumann, and Ravel, I wanted to write one of my own.

Each movement speaks a different language. The first grows out of my long-standing interest in Romantic piano writing, with singing melodies and shifting tonal centers. The second continues in that direction, with denser chromatic harmony and more virtuosic textures. The third, still connected to the first two, moves toward a more rhythmically unstable and harmonically ambiguous world, with Ravel and Shostakovich somewhere in the background. It is also the first piece in which I thought carefully about the form of each movement before writing a note.

The concerto was written during a particularly demanding academic year, and for a long stretch it was hard to sit down at the piano and keep going. What emerged gathers many ideas that grew alongside everything else I was doing, and it carries my character and emotions from that time.
