Research & Technology

Machine learning and music information retrieval, mostly on questions a pianist runs into at the keyboard.

Automatic Piano Fingering Estimation

Senior capstone, advised by Chris Donahue. 2026 to present.

At this data scale, the task rewards local inductive bias, not long-range context.

A three-part study on the PIG dataset: five model families benchmarked under one protocol, with every decision rule written down before the data existed and the test set evaluated once per part. Task-aligned pretraining trained to convergence was the first configuration to beat the hand-crafted CRF; on polyphonic music, a one-note attention window selected on validation beat full attention and a retrained published baseline. Write-up in progress for publication.

Score Following and Performance-to-Score Alignment

Research assistant to Roger Dannenberg. 2026 to present.

A score follower is only as good as the aligned data it is judged on, and the clock it is judged by.

For the opera Felicità I built an ASAP-style performance-to-score alignment dataset: automated draft alignments, automatic flagging of likely errors, and by-ear correction of every section. I compared ASAP and nASAP alignments across 1,065 performances, found and fixed a bug in the group's score-following evaluation reader that changed the reported error distributions, and am now developing rule-based online prediction of performance time under causal timing constraints, evaluated against the HMM and Accomplice followers.

SATB Harmony Grader and Solver

Independent project. Python, Flask, VexFlow.

Obeying every rule is the easy part; the solver also has to sound like a student who listens.

A rule-based grader that adapts John Paul Ito's harmony curriculum, with permission, into eleven categories of diagnostic feedback on voice leading, spacing, parallels, and tendency-tone resolution, paired with a two-stage heuristic solver that searches rule-valid realizations while optimizing musical preferences: smooth voice leading, cadence-aware soprano planning, and phrase-level melodic shape. Teacher and student interfaces in Flask, with VexFlow notation and SoundFont playback.

About

Hi, I'm Daniel! I'm finishing two degrees at Carnegie Mellon, a B.S. in Music and Technology on the computer science track and a B.F.A. in Music Composition, and most of what I do lives where the two meet.

On the technology side I work in music information retrieval. My capstone, advised by Chris Donahue, asks whether a model can learn how pianists choose fingerings; in Roger Dannenberg's group I work on score following and performance-to-score alignment. Underneath both is the question I find most exciting: what does a performer add that the score never wrote down?

As a composer (I study with James Whipple) I write music that moves: a continuous, tidal sense of motion, plenty of dramatic tension, and ideas that get transformed and fragmented rather than lined up side by side. My harmonic language is freely chromatic and color-conscious. The Piano Concerto in C-sharp minor is the fullest statement of all that so far, and the Piano Scherzo in G gets its premiere with the CMU Philharmonic in March 2027!

And I'm a pianist, which is where everything started. I study with Sung-Im Kim, have played the solo part in my own premieres, and happily show up at the keyboard for my fellow composers' pieces too. Yes, I decide fingerings every day. It keeps the research honest.
